# CineGraph deep-research pilot

This is the first readable outcome of CineGraph's narrative research layer.
It is based on retained, versioned English Wikipedia pages rather than a
generic movie summary. The system stores each article as section-bound passages
and attaches every answer below to those passages.

**Evidence key:**

- **Fact** — directly recorded source information.
- **Narrative extraction** — a bounded description of what the story or
  production account says.
- **Attributed interpretation** — a critic, scholar, publication or reception
  view. It is not CineGraph declaring a single truth.

All pilot answers remain `review_required` pending editorial review. That is
intentional: an answer can be useful now without pretending a machine has
settled film history.

## The Dark Knight (2008)

Source: [English Wikipedia, revision 1368945348](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Dark_Knight&oldid=1368945348), captured 2026-08-12, CC BY-SA 4.0. Canonical identity: Wikidata `Q163872`.

The captured article yielded **72 readable passages in 24 sections**, carrying
**490 citation markers**. CineGraph attaches eight research answers to 67
specific passages.

### What actually drives the story

**Narrative extraction.** Batman, Gordon and Harvey Dent begin with a civic
project: dismantling Gotham's organised crime. Dent supplies the legal public
face that Batman cannot. The Joker turns that campaign into an escalation
system: every answer—public identity, Rachel/Dent, the ferry choice—raises the
cost to the city's law, trust and moral limits.

The useful screenwriting insight is not “this is a superhero film.” It is that
the film puts three incompatible public symbols under pressure:

| Symbol | Dramatic promise | How Joker attacks it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Batman | extralegal order with a moral code | force him toward murder, surveillance and public disgrace |
| Harvey Dent | lawful civic hope | prove that the ideal public servant can be corrupted |
| Joker | chaos without a stable identity | turn each moral constraint into a weapon |

This makes *The Dark Knight* comparable to another film only through an
explicit lens—such as an antagonist that weaponises civic trust—not merely
because both have a villain or share a genre.

### Nolan's creative and formal choices

**Fact / documented production intent.** The production and writing account
describes a grounded crime-drama frame, with Dent developed as a central
dramatic figure and Joker intentionally denied a settled origin story. That is
a far more useful comparison handle than “dark superhero movie.”

- The project keeps *Batman Begins*' recognisable contemporary world while
  drawing on crime-drama conventions.
- Dent is not simply a supporting villain-in-waiting; he is the legal promise
  Batman hopes can replace him.
- The Joker's absent backstory is a deliberate uncertainty mechanism.
- IMAX photography and practical effects/stunts are documented production
  choices; they can support craft comparisons with other large-format or
  practical-spectacle films.
- The score differentiates Dent, Batman and Joker rather than treating music as
  one undifferentiated mood.

### Heath Ledger: the evidence-backed career record

**Fact, with attributed reception kept separate.** CineGraph records this as a
dated chain rather than a legend:

1. Ledger played the Joker.
2. He died before the film's release.
3. He received posthumous major acting recognition, including the Academy Award
   for Best Supporting Actor.
4. Critical praise and later “greatest villain” rankings are retained as named
   critics, polls or publications—not as an unqualified CineGraph verdict.

The defensible card is: **a posthumous-award landmark performance with a
documented critical-reception trail.**

### Industry, cultural impact and disagreement

**Facts:** the page documents the interactive Dent/Joker alternate-reality
marketing campaign, record commercial performance, a worldwide gross above
$1 billion, and United States National Film Registry selection in 2020.

**Attributed interpretation:** the article records a widespread account that
the film became a modern-superhero blueprint, alongside the counter-reading
that later films often copied its grim tone rather than its underlying dramatic
construction. Both views must appear together.

The so-called “Dark Knight Rule” is therefore stored as **causal context**, not
a simplistic “this film changed the Academy” fact: the article documents the
Best Picture nomination debate and the later nominee-field expansion, but does
not prove a one-film cause.

### Thematic readings should coexist

The article's `Themes and analysis` sections yield four distinct, sourced
routes:

- escalation, terrorism and the war-on-terror analogy;
- emergency surveillance versus civil liberties;
- morality under coercion, especially the ferry dilemma;
- Dent as civic symbol and the ending's “noble lie.”

They are stored as parallel attributed readings, not reduced to an opaque
keyword such as `politics` or `morality`.

### Proven direct lineage

*Batman Begins* → *The Dark Knight* → *The Dark Knight Rises* is a factual
trilogy route. It supports better questions than a generic related-films score:
what civic or moral pressure does each installment inherit, and what does the
next installment transform?

## What this changes

The system no longer has to invent relationships from shared cast, genre or
year. It can pose and eventually answer questions such as:

- Which films turn an antagonist into a test of a public institution rather
  than only a protagonist's physical opponent?
- Which adaptations deliberately move their source material's central concern?
- Which action films are designed first as visual movement systems?
- Where does a film's cultural impact come from a documented recognition,
  commercial event, technique, reception movement or direct lineage—and where
  is it only a critic's reading?

Embeddings come after this foundation. They may retrieve similar licensed
passages as candidates, but cannot make a relationship appear as proven without
the reviewed evidence above.
